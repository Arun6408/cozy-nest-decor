Empty walls make a room feel unfinished, but filling them with mass-produced art can feel impersonal. Handmade pieces from independent makers give your walls character and a story. Here are ten ideas to try.

## 1. Build a small gallery wall

Start with three to five frames in the same finish, mixing prints, a small mirror and a textile. Lay everything out on the floor first, then hang the largest piece at eye level and build around it.

## 2. Hang a woven basket wall

A cluster of flat woven baskets in different sizes covers a large wall for less than one big canvas. It adds texture and works especially well above a bed or sofa.

## 3. Use a round mirror to bounce light

A large round mirror opposite a window makes a small room feel brighter and bigger. Rattan or wood frames add warmth that metal frames don't.

## 4. Add floating shelves for styling

Floating shelves let you change your wall decor with the seasons. Style them with small plants, a few books and one or two ceramic pieces, leaving some empty space.

## 5. Frame a textile or vintage scarf

A framed piece of fabric, such as a block-printed textile or vintage scarf, is unexpected and full of color. It's a great way to bring pattern into a neutral room.

## 6. Hang a wall planter

Wall planters bring greenery to rooms with no floor space. Trailing pothos or string-of-pearls look lovely spilling over the edge.

## 7. Try a large-scale abstract print

One oversized print can do more than many small ones. Choose colors already in your pillows or rug so the art ties the room together.

<Tip>Hang art so its center sits about 57 inches (145 cm) from the floor, the height galleries use.</Tip>

## 8. Display a handmade wall hanging

Macramé, weaving and punch-needle pieces bring softness to hard walls. They also help absorb a little sound in echoey rooms.

## 9. Create a plate wall

Vintage or handmade plates hung in a loose cluster make a charming wall in a kitchen or dining nook. Use adhesive plate hangers so you don't need to drill each one.

## 10. Lean, don't hang

If you rent or can't decide, lean framed art on a sideboard, mantel or picture ledge. Layering two or three frames of different sizes looks relaxed and is easy to change.

Save this list to your wall decor board and start with the idea that fits your room best.
