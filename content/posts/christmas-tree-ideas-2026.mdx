This year's favorite Christmas trees look classic and cozy: deep green branches, warm white light, rich burgundy and gold, and natural textures. The good news is that the look is easy to copy with a handful of pieces. Here are ten ideas, in the order you'd actually decorate.

## 1. Start with a full, pre-lit tree

A pre-lit tree saves the most frustrating job of all. Look for warm white lights and plenty of branch tips so the tree looks full from every side.

## 2. Fluff every branch before you decorate

Spend 20 minutes spreading the inner and outer tips on each branch, working from the bottom up. It's the step that makes an artificial tree look expensive, and it hides the center pole.

<Tip>Shape the branches with the lights on. You'll spot dark gaps instantly and can bend a branch to fill them.</Tip>

## 3. Add a second layer of warm light

For a really glowing tree, tuck an extra strand of warm white lights deep inside the branches. Light from the center gives the tree depth, so it glows rather than sparkles on the surface.

## 4. Make big velvet bows

Oversized velvet bows are the signature of this year's classic trees. Wired ribbon holds its shape, so the bows stay crisp all season.

## 5. Cascade ribbon down the tree

Use the same ribbon in long, loose waves from the top to the bottom. Tuck it into the branches every 12 inches or so and let the gold glitter side flip out now and then.

## 6. Drape a wood bead garland

A bead garland adds a handmade, rustic touch that balances the shine of glass ornaments. Swag it in shallow curves between the ribbon cascades.

## 7. Keep ornaments to four colors

Stick to burgundy, red, green and gold. A tight palette makes even a mix of new and old ornaments look planned. Hang the biggest ones first, deep in the branches, then fill in with smaller ones.

## 8. Crown it with a glowing star

A lighted star pulls the whole look together and glows beautifully when the room lights are off.

## 9. Finish the base

Wrap a few gift boxes in kraft paper and the same burgundy ribbon, or use a tree collar. A tidy base makes the tree look finished, even before the real gifts arrive.

## 10. Repeat the palette around the room

Tie a matching velvet bow on a wreath or the mantel, and drape a second bead garland on the stairs. Repeating colors and textures makes the whole room feel styled, not just the tree.

<Tip>Take a photo of the tree in black and white. Any bare or crowded spots stand out right away.</Tip>
