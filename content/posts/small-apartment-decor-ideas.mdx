A small apartment can feel cozy instead of cramped. The trick is to make every piece earn its place, keep sightlines open and use light to your advantage. These twelve ideas work in studios, one-bedrooms and rentals.

## 1. Hang a large mirror opposite a window

A big mirror doubles the daylight and visually extends the room. Leaning a tall floor mirror is renter-friendly and just as effective.

## 2. Choose furniture with legs

Sofas, chairs and cabinets raised on legs show more floor, which tricks the eye into reading the room as larger.

## 3. Go vertical with storage

Use the height of your walls. Tall bookcases, over-the-door organizers and wall shelves free up precious floor space.

## 4. Pick multi-use furniture

A storage ottoman, a nesting coffee table or a bench with baskets underneath does two jobs in one footprint.

## 5. Keep a light, consistent color palette

Light walls and a palette of two or three soft colors make a small space feel calm and connected. Add warmth with wood and textiles instead of dark paint.

## 6. Define zones with rugs

In a studio, a rug under the sofa and another under the bed create separate "rooms" without walls. Keep both in the same color family.

## 7. Use a room divider that lets light through

An open bookshelf or a slatted screen separates sleeping and living areas while letting light pass.

## 8. Hang curtains high and wide

Mount the rod close to the ceiling and extend it past the frame. Windows look taller and the whole room feels lifted.

## 9. Swap the coffee table for a pouf or tray stool

A pouf or a small round table takes less visual space than a rectangular coffee table and is easier to move.

## 10. Add wall-mounted or plug-in lighting

Plug-in wall sconces free up side tables and add a cozy glow without hiring an electrician.

## 11. Edit decor to a few larger pieces

Many small objects read as clutter in a small room. A few larger pieces, such as one big plant or one statement print, look calmer.

## 12. Hide the mess in matching baskets

Matching baskets on open shelves turn visual clutter into a tidy, textured display.

<Tip>Do a 10-minute reset every evening. In a small space, tidy surfaces make the biggest difference.</Tip>
